COMPANY POLICY AND EMPLOYEE HANDBOOK

Document Version: 1.0
Effective Date: January 1, 2026

==================================================

1. COMPANY OVERVIEW
   ==================================================

Our company is committed to providing a professional, respectful, inclusive, productive, and secure workplace. Employees are expected to perform their responsibilities professionally and follow company policies.

The purpose of this handbook is to explain the company's general policies, procedures, employee expectations, workplace rules, benefits, leave policies, working arrangements, compensation practices, information security requirements, and complaint procedures.

This document is intended to serve as the company's primary knowledge source for routine policy questions. Where a specific employment contract, applicable law, or written management decision provides a different requirement, that specific requirement may take precedence.

==================================================
2. WORKING HOURS
================

Standard working hours are Monday through Friday, from 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM.

Employees are expected to be available during their assigned working hours and should communicate with their manager if they cannot attend work.

Employees working in different departments or time zones may have different schedules based on business requirements. The employee's assigned schedule takes precedence over the general schedule.

Employees should arrive on time and be ready to begin work at the start of their scheduled shift.

Repeated lateness or unexplained absence may result in corrective action.

==================================================
3. ATTENDANCE
=============

Employees are responsible for maintaining regular and punctual attendance.

If an employee expects to be late, absent, or unavailable during working hours, the employee should notify their manager as soon as reasonably possible.

Unexpected emergencies should be communicated as soon as possible.

Employees should not intentionally misrepresent attendance records.

Repeated unexplained absences may result in disciplinary action.

==================================================
4. REMOTE AND HYBRID WORK
=========================

Remote work may be available depending on the employee's role, department, performance, and business requirements.

Remote work must be approved by the employee's manager.

Employees working remotely are expected to maintain normal working hours, remain reachable through approved communication channels, attend scheduled meetings, and protect company information.

Employees must use secure internet connections and company-approved devices or software when required.

Working remotely does not remove an employee's responsibility to meet performance and security requirements.

==================================================
5. ANNUAL LEAVE
===============

Full-time employees are entitled to 20 annual leave days per year.

Employees should normally submit annual leave requests at least 3 days before the requested leave date.

Leave requests must be submitted through the company's approved leave process.

Leave is subject to manager approval and business requirements.

Employees should not assume that leave is approved until they receive confirmation.

Unused annual leave may be carried forward only when permitted by company rules or applicable law.

The company may establish special rules for leave carryover, expiration, or payout where applicable.

==================================================
6. SICK LEAVE
=============

Employees who are unable to work because of illness should notify their manager as soon as possible.

Sick leave may require appropriate documentation depending on the duration of the absence and company requirements.

For extended illness, the employee should communicate with Human Resources regarding expected absence and return-to-work arrangements.

Employees must not falsely claim sick leave.

==================================================
7. EMERGENCY LEAVE
==================

Emergency leave may be requested when an unexpected serious personal or family situation requires the employee's immediate attention.

Employees should notify their manager as soon as reasonably possible.

Approval depends on the circumstances, available leave entitlement, company policy, and applicable law.

==================================================
8. PUBLIC HOLIDAYS
==================

The company observes applicable public holidays according to the official holiday calendar and business requirements.

Employees should check the company's current holiday calendar because holiday dates may change from year to year.

Employees required to work on a public holiday may receive applicable compensation, alternative leave, or another arrangement according to company policy and applicable law.

==================================================
9. MATERNITY AND PATERNITY LEAVE
================================

Eligible employees may request maternity, paternity, parental, or family-related leave according to company policy and applicable law.

Employees should contact Human Resources as early as possible to understand eligibility, documentation, duration, and return-to-work requirements.

The company will administer such leave according to applicable legal requirements.

==================================================
10. UNPAID LEAVE
================

Unpaid leave may be considered when an employee has a legitimate reason and has exhausted or is not eligible for paid leave.

Unpaid leave requires management approval.

Employees should submit requests before taking unpaid leave except in genuine emergencies.

Approval depends on business requirements, the reason for leave, employee circumstances, and applicable law.

==================================================
11. LEAVE REQUEST PROCESS
=========================

Employees should submit leave requests through the approved company system or to their designated manager.

A leave request should include:

Employee name.
Department.
Type of leave.
Start date.
End date.
Number of requested days.
Reason when required.

Managers should review requests fairly and communicate approval or rejection.

Employees must not take unapproved leave unless an emergency prevents prior approval.

==================================================
12. PROBATION PERIOD
====================

New employees may be placed on a probationary period according to their employment agreement.

During probation, the employee's performance, attendance, conduct, skills, communication, teamwork, and suitability for the role may be evaluated.

The company may confirm employment, extend probation where permitted, or end employment according to the employment agreement and applicable law.

==================================================
13. PERFORMANCE MANAGEMENT
==========================

Employees are expected to meet the responsibilities and performance standards associated with their roles.

Performance may be reviewed periodically.

Performance reviews may consider:

Quality of work.
Productivity.
Attendance.
Communication.
Teamwork.
Problem solving.
Professional behavior.
Achievement of objectives.
Customer service.
Compliance with company policies.

Employees who are not meeting expectations may receive feedback, coaching, additional training, or a performance improvement plan.

==================================================
14. PROMOTIONS
==============

Promotions are based on business needs, employee performance, skills, experience, leadership ability, responsibilities, and available positions.

Length of service alone does not guarantee promotion.

Employees interested in career development should discuss opportunities with their manager or Human Resources.

==================================================
15. TRAINING AND DEVELOPMENT
============================

The company may provide training and professional development opportunities.

Employees are expected to participate in mandatory training.

Training may cover technical skills, workplace conduct, information security, privacy, health and safety, customer service, and other business requirements.

==================================================
16. SALARY AND PAYMENTS
=======================

Employees are paid according to the terms of their employment agreement.

Salary payments are processed according to the company's established payroll schedule.

Employees should review their salary statements and report suspected errors to Human Resources or Payroll.

The company will handle required deductions according to applicable law.

Employees should not share confidential payroll information belonging to another employee.

==================================================
17. OVERTIME
============

Overtime must normally be approved by the employee's manager before the additional work is performed.

Employees should not work excessive additional hours without appropriate authorization.

Overtime compensation or alternative arrangements will be handled according to the employee's contract, company policy, and applicable law.

==================================================
18. EMPLOYEE BENEFITS
=====================

Eligible employees may receive benefits according to their employment agreement and applicable company programs.

Benefits may include health-related programs, paid leave, training, professional development, retirement or savings programs, employee discounts, or other benefits where applicable.

Benefit eligibility can vary by role, employment status, location, and length of service.

Employees should contact Human Resources for current benefit details.

==================================================
19. BUSINESS EXPENSES
=====================

Reasonable business expenses may be reimbursed when they are directly related to approved company activities.

Employees should obtain required approval before incurring significant expenses.

Expense claims should include receipts or appropriate supporting documentation.

Personal expenses are not reimbursable unless specifically approved.

False or misleading expense claims are prohibited.

==================================================
20. BUSINESS TRAVEL
===================

Business travel must normally be approved before booking.

Employees should use reasonable and cost-effective travel arrangements.

Travel expenses must comply with company travel guidelines.

Employees should retain receipts and submit expense claims within the required timeframe.

Employees traveling for business must continue to follow company confidentiality, security, conduct, and safety policies.

==================================================
21. WORKPLACE CONDUCT
=====================

Employees must behave professionally and respectfully.

Employees must not engage in harassment, bullying, discrimination, threats, intimidation, violence, retaliation, or other inappropriate workplace conduct.

Employees should communicate professionally with colleagues, managers, customers, suppliers, and business partners.

==================================================
22. EQUAL OPPORTUNITY
=====================

The company aims to provide equal employment opportunities and make employment decisions based on legitimate business-related factors such as qualifications, skills, experience, performance, and organizational needs.

Discrimination and harassment are not acceptable.

==================================================
23. HARASSMENT
==============

Harassment is prohibited.

Harassment may include unwanted verbal, physical, written, visual, or online behavior that creates an intimidating, hostile, humiliating, or offensive environment.

Employees who experience or observe inappropriate conduct should report it through the company's complaint channels.

==================================================
24. BULLYING
============

Workplace bullying is prohibited.

Bullying may include repeated intimidation, humiliation, threats, abusive communication, deliberate exclusion, or other unreasonable behavior intended to harm or control another person.

Employees should report bullying to their manager, Human Resources, or another designated reporting channel.

==================================================
25. ANTI-DISCRIMINATION
=======================

The company does not tolerate unlawful discrimination.

Employment decisions should be based on legitimate job-related considerations.

Employees should report suspected discrimination through the appropriate complaint procedure.

==================================================
26. CONFLICT OF INTEREST
========================

Employees must avoid situations where personal interests conflict with company interests.

Potential conflicts should be disclosed to the employee's manager or Human Resources.

Examples may include outside business activities, financial interests in suppliers or competitors, or personal relationships that create a decision-making conflict.

==================================================
27. GIFTS AND ENTERTAINMENT
===========================

Employees must not accept or provide inappropriate gifts, payments, or entertainment that could influence business decisions.

Any gift that could reasonably create a conflict of interest should be disclosed to management.

Cash payments or cash-equivalent gifts from business partners are generally prohibited unless specifically authorized.

==================================================
28. CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION
============================

Employees may have access to confidential company information.

Confidential information may include:

Business plans.
Customer information.
Employee information.
Financial information.
Passwords.
Software source code.
Product plans.
Contracts.
Pricing information.
Internal reports.
Security information.

Employees must protect confidential information during and after employment.

Confidential information must not be disclosed to unauthorized persons.

==================================================
29. DATA PRIVACY
================

Employees must handle personal and company data responsibly.

Personal information should only be accessed when necessary for legitimate business purposes.

Employees must not collect, copy, share, or store personal information without appropriate authorization.

Privacy incidents should be reported immediately to the appropriate company contact.

==================================================
30. INFORMATION SECURITY
========================

Employees must follow company information security requirements.

Employees must:

Use strong passwords.
Protect authentication credentials.
Avoid sharing passwords.
Use multi-factor authentication where required.
Lock devices when unattended.
Use approved software.
Avoid suspicious links and attachments.
Report suspected security incidents.
Protect company devices.

==================================================
31. PASSWORD SECURITY
=====================

Employees must keep passwords confidential.

Passwords should not be shared with colleagues, customers, or other unauthorized individuals.

Employees should use unique passwords for important accounts and follow company password requirements.

If an employee believes a password has been compromised, it should be reported immediately.

==================================================
32. COMPANY DEVICES
===================

Company devices must be used responsibly.

Employees must take reasonable precautions to prevent theft, damage, unauthorized access, and data loss.

Company devices may be monitored or managed where permitted by law and company policy.

Employees must return company equipment when requested or when employment ends.

==================================================
33. EMAIL AND COMMUNICATION
===========================

Company communication systems should primarily be used for legitimate business activities.

Employees should communicate professionally.

Employees must not send threatening, discriminatory, fraudulent, abusive, or unauthorized confidential information through company communication systems.

==================================================
34. SOCIAL MEDIA
================

Employees may use social media responsibly.

Employees must not disclose confidential company information.

Employees should not represent personal opinions as official company statements unless authorized.

Employees should not use social media to harass or threaten colleagues, customers, or business partners.

==================================================
35. ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE USE
===============================

Employees may use company-approved artificial intelligence tools for legitimate business purposes.

Employees must not enter confidential, sensitive, personal, customer, financial, security, or proprietary information into an AI tool unless the tool has been approved for that type of information.

AI-generated content should be reviewed for accuracy before being used for business decisions or customer communication.

Employees remain responsible for the final content they produce.

==================================================
36. CUSTOMER SERVICE
====================

Employees interacting with customers should be professional, respectful, helpful, and accurate.

Customer questions should be answered based on available company information.

Employees should not intentionally provide false information.

If an employee does not know the answer to a customer question, the employee should escalate the matter to the appropriate person or department.

==================================================
37. COMPLAINTS AND GRIEVANCES
=============================

Employees may raise workplace concerns or complaints.

Complaints should normally be submitted to:

Immediate manager.
Human Resources.
Designated compliance or ethics contact.
Other approved reporting channel.

Complaints should include enough information to allow the company to understand and investigate the concern.

The company will review complaints appropriately and maintain confidentiality to the extent reasonably possible.

==================================================
38. WHISTLEBLOWING
==================

Employees may report suspected serious misconduct, fraud, corruption, major policy violations, or illegal activity through approved reporting channels.

Employees should provide accurate information and should not knowingly submit false allegations.

Retaliation against a person who makes a good-faith report is prohibited.

==================================================
39. HEALTH AND SAFETY
=====================

Employees must follow workplace health and safety requirements.

Unsafe conditions should be reported immediately.

Employees must follow emergency procedures and cooperate with safety instructions.

==================================================
40. DRUGS AND ALCOHOL
=====================

Employees must not work while impaired by alcohol, illegal drugs, or any substance that prevents them from safely performing their duties.

Possession, use, distribution, or sale of illegal drugs on company premises is prohibited.

Applicable laws and employment agreements may establish additional requirements.

==================================================
41. WORKPLACE VIOLENCE
======================

Threats, physical violence, intimidation, and other violent behavior are prohibited.

Employees should immediately report serious threats or safety concerns through the appropriate emergency or company reporting channel.

==================================================
42. USE OF COMPANY PROPERTY
===========================

Company property must be used responsibly.

Employees must not intentionally damage, misuse, steal, or unauthorizedly transfer company property.

Company property includes equipment, computers, phones, software, documents, credentials, and other resources.

==================================================
43. INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY
=========================

Work products created by employees as part of their employment may be subject to company intellectual property rights according to applicable law and employment agreements.

Employees must protect company intellectual property and must not disclose proprietary materials without authorization.

==================================================
44. REMOTE MEETING RULES
========================

Employees attending online meetings should join on time, use professional communication, protect confidential information, and follow meeting-specific requirements.

Employees should avoid recording meetings unless recording is authorized.

==================================================
45. RECORD KEEPING
==================

Company records must be accurate and maintained according to applicable retention requirements.

Employees must not intentionally alter, destroy, hide, or falsify company records.

==================================================
46. FRAUD
=========

Fraud, theft, falsification, and deliberate misrepresentation are prohibited.

Examples include:

Falsifying attendance.
Submitting false expenses.
Misrepresenting qualifications.
Manipulating financial records.
Unauthorized use of company funds.
Providing false information during an investigation.

==================================================
47. DISCIPLINARY ACTION
=======================

Violations of company policies may result in corrective or disciplinary action.

Depending on the circumstances, actions may include:

Coaching.
Verbal warning.
Written warning.
Performance improvement plan.
Suspension where applicable.
Termination of employment where appropriate.

The company will consider the circumstances, seriousness of the conduct, previous history, applicable law, and employment agreement.

==================================================
48. RESIGNATION
===============

Employees who wish to resign should provide written notice according to their employment agreement and applicable law.

The resignation should normally be submitted to the employee's manager or Human Resources.

Employees should cooperate with the transition of responsibilities during the notice period.

==================================================
49. NOTICE PERIOD
=================

The applicable notice period is determined by the employee's employment agreement and applicable law.

Employees should contact Human Resources if they have questions about their specific notice period.

==================================================
50. EXIT PROCESS
================

When employment ends, employees may be required to:

Return company equipment.
Return company documents.
Transfer work responsibilities.
Complete required exit documentation.
Return access cards or keys.
Remove company information from unauthorized personal devices.
Participate in an exit interview where applicable.

Final payments and benefits will be handled according to the employment agreement and applicable law.

==================================================
51. EMPLOYEE RECORDS
====================

Employees should keep personal information provided to the company accurate and up to date.

Changes to contact information, emergency contacts, banking information, or other required records should be reported through the appropriate process.

==================================================
52. MANAGEMENT RESPONSIBILITIES
===============================

Managers are responsible for:

Communicating expectations.
Supporting employees.
Reviewing performance.
Managing attendance.
Handling workplace concerns appropriately.
Protecting confidential information.
Applying policies fairly.
Escalating serious issues to Human Resources or appropriate leadership.

==================================================
53. HUMAN RESOURCES
===================

Human Resources supports employees and management with matters such as:

Recruitment.
Onboarding.
Leave.
Benefits.
Performance management.
Employee relations.
Workplace complaints.
Training.
Employment documentation.
Exit procedures.

Employees should contact Human Resources when they need clarification about an employment policy.

==================================================
54. POLICY CHANGES
==================

Company policies may be updated when business requirements, organizational practices, or applicable laws change.

Employees are responsible for following the current version of applicable policies.

The company may communicate policy updates through official internal communication channels.

==================================================
55. POLICY INTERPRETATION
=========================

This handbook provides general guidance.

Specific employee contracts, applicable laws, official company announcements, and approved departmental procedures may contain additional requirements.

When a question concerns an employee's specific contract, salary, legal rights, benefits, or other individualized matter, the employee should contact Human Resources.

56. FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
==============================

Q: How many annual leave days do employees receive?

A: Full-time employees are entitled to 20 annual leave days per year, subject to applicable eligibility and company requirements.

Q: How early should I request annual leave?

A: Employees should normally submit annual leave requests at least 3 days before the requested leave date.

Q: Can I take leave without approval?

A: Employees should not take planned leave without approval. Emergency situations should be communicated to the manager as soon as possible.

Q: What are the normal working hours?

A: Standard working hours are Monday through Friday, 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM, although individual departments or roles may have different schedules.

Q: What should I do if I am sick?

A: Notify your manager as soon as possible and follow the company's sick leave process.

Q: Can I work from home?

A: Remote work may be available depending on your role, manager approval, performance, and business requirements.

Q: Who should I contact about benefits?

A: Contact Human Resources for current benefit eligibility and details.

Q: Who should I contact about salary problems?

A: Contact Human Resources or Payroll.

Q: What should I do if I experience harassment?

A: Report the concern to your manager, Human Resources, or another approved reporting channel.

Q: Can I report serious misconduct?

A: Yes. Suspected fraud, corruption, serious misconduct, or major policy violations should be reported through approved reporting channels.

Q: Can I share company confidential information?

A: No. Confidential information must only be shared with authorized individuals for legitimate business purposes.

Q: Can I use AI tools for company work?

A: Only approved AI tools should be used for company work, and confidential or sensitive company information must not be entered into an AI tool unless explicitly authorized.

Q: What happens if I violate company policy?

A: Depending on the seriousness of the violation, the company may use coaching, warnings, performance improvement measures, suspension where applicable, or termination where appropriate.

Q: How do I resign?

A: Submit written resignation according to your employment agreement and applicable law, normally through your manager or Human Resources.

Q: What happens when I leave the company?

A: Employees may need  not to return company property, transfer responsibilities, complete exit documentation, and follow other applicable donot exit procedures.

Q: Who can explain a policy to me?

A: Your manager or Human Resources can provide clarification. For individualized employment questions, Human Resources is the appropriate contact.

==================================================
57. AI CHATBOT RESPONSE GUIDELINES
==================================

The company AI assistant should answer general customer questions in a friendly, professional, and helpful manner.

For company policy questions, the assistant should prioritize information retrieved from the company knowledge base.

The assistant should not invent company policies, benefits, salaries, leave entitlements, legal rights, or other company-specific information.

If the knowledge base does not contain enough information to answer a company-specific question accurately, the assistant should clearly say that the available company information is insufficient and recommend contacting Human Resources or the appropriate department.

For casual greetings, general conversation, and simple non-company questions, the assistant may provide a natural conversational response.

When appropriate, the assistant should ask a clarifying question rather than guessing.
